## 1. What the bot actually is

A 15-min polling loop (agent.py, 2,530 lines) that:
- computes a 4-line plain-EMA ribbon (8/13/21/55 bars at 30m live) from Yahoo data;
- smart-skips ~90% of cycles; on wake, shells out to `claude -p` (Haiku for execution
  cycles, Opus for research/postmortems) with skills/*.md as the system prompt;
- the model reads/places orders through the Robinhood MCP; Python then **re-reads the
  broker independently** (the model's self-reported footer has lied before) and
  deterministically force-sells anything in the hard must-sell set;
- daily 9:20 ET Opus research writes `weekend_picks_*.md` (current mandate v25:
  concentrated top-3 multi-timeframe momentum rotation, EMA-BUY + >SMA200 gated);
- a learning loop (postmortems → source weights → skill_5 strategy rewrites, all
  versioned+rollbackable) mutates strategy.json (now v30);
- two paper-options sleeves (EMA shadow + momentum shadow) build a spread-inclusive
  shadow record; real options hard-gated off below $1k account value.

Safety machinery that genuinely works and must be preserved: broker reconciliation
(no phantom closes), deterministic force_sell, leverage-adjusted stops & sizing in
Python (not prompts), strategy/skill versioning + rollback, change-event log,
never-fabricate-signals (INSUFFICIENT_DATA blocks trading).

## 3. Weakness catalog (ranked by expected damage)

1. **Process-resident risk controls (CRITICAL).** Stops/trailing stops exist only while a
   laptop process runs. Proven cost: MU −17% through a −10% stop. No GTC stop orders at the
   broker, no watchdog/heartbeat, alert webhook unset. *A 15-min poller also cannot beat an
   overnight gap even when it IS running.*
2. **No measured edge in the signal layer.** The bot's own backtests: ribbon loses to
   buy-and-hold on 23/29 names (10y); clean live expectancy negative; the v25 rotation's
   backtest is ~20–28%/yr at **~69% max drawdown** — a bull-decade artifact with a ruin-adjacent
   DD profile. Confidence scores (60–100) are model-vibes, uncalibrated: conf-80 entries have
   lost repeatedly (AMD −5%, HOOD −2.6%, MU now −17%).
3. **Prompt-level rules don't bind.** LR003a (overextension guard) is in the skill file, yet
   the Jun 26 research bought MU **+327%/6mo, the day after a +27% earnings pop**, writing
   "extended after the earnings pop" in its own caveat. Every rule that has actually held is
   enforced in Python; every rule that has been violated lives only in a prompt.
4. **Model-in-the-execution-path fragility.** Order placement requires `claude -p` to be up,
   under session caps, and obedient. Observed failure modes: fabricated footer (phantom
   sells), denied Skill invocation stalls, session-limit no-op cycles, rc=1 blindness. The
   broker-reconciliation + force_sell layer mitigates but the dependency remains for the
   *entry* side and for every forced exit (force_sell is itself a claude -p call).
5. **Capital accounting breaks on deposits** (see §2) — sizing denominator silently wrong.
6. **Churn drag.** v25 rotation closed 4 names in one cycle for −$1.3 net; on a $400 account
   with fractional shares this is pure friction against no proven edge. skill_5 has already
   burned Opus calls re-reviewing the same trades 9 times (queue re-fire bug, since fixed).
7. **Options shadow is mis-designed for the account**: ATM calls on four-digit underlyings
   produce oversized-flagged records that prove nothing about what a $1k account would trade;
   momentum sleeve thresholds so tight it never trades. Shadow record is accumulating noise,
   not evidence.
8. **Single data vendor** (Yahoo unauthenticated endpoints) with no cross-check; extended-hours
   closes feed stop checks (a pre-market wick can trigger a "stop" the regular session never hit).
9. **Test drift**: 255/256 pass; `test_full_flow_opens_momentum_shadow` fails (stubbed screen
   no longer clears the v25 gates — the test wasn't updated with the config).
10. **Cost**: ~$65–90/mo of model calls against a $400 account (~20%/mo drag if it were paid
    at list; currently inside the Pro plan, but it bounds how chatty the loop can get).

## 4. What the record supports keeping

- The **deterministic Python risk rail** (sizing, stops, sector caps, reconciliation,
  force_sell, versioning). It's the only part that has never lied.
- The **daily research file contract** (`### #N — SYMBOL | Confidence`) as the human-readable
  layer — but demoted from "the strategy" to "context".
- The **shadow-first promotion discipline** (paper record before real dollars) — the concept
  is right even though the current shadow implementation measures the wrong thing.
- The learning-loop *plumbing* (change events, postmortems) as audit trail.

Everything else — the EMA ribbon as the entry edge, model-scored confidence as the sizing
input, prompt-enforced entry gates, the 100%/mo (now 25%/mo) goal framing — is unsupported
by the measured record and should be replaced by strategies proven in backtest and enforced
in Python.
